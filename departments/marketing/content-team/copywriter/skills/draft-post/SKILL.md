# draft-post

Writes one post. Your voice, one reader, one job.

## Read these first, every time

Before writing a single line, read the shared brain at the top of this repo:

- `business.md`: what you sell and who buys it
- `brand/icp.md`: the person you're writing to
- `brand/personas/`: if the owner named a persona (or there's only one), write to that one file's reader
- `brand/voice.md`: how you actually sound
- `brand/contrarian-take.md`: the belief that makes this brand worth following
- `brand/channels.md`: the funnel split; which job (give / prove / invite) is even in demand this week
- `knowledge/topics.md`: the topic list and The Four Questions every post answers
- `knowledge/hooks.md`: the openers that already won for this owner

If any of those are missing or thin, say so and ask for what's missing instead of guessing. A post written without them is a post about nobody.

## Ask before you write

1. **What's the post about?** One sentence is fine.
2. **Which job is it doing?** Getting noticed by someone who's never heard of them, building trust with someone who already knows them, or asking for the sale.
3. **Where's it going?** The platform changes the length and the shape, nothing else.

Ask one at a time and wait. If the answer to #1 is vague, ask for the specific thing that happened, not the topic.

If plan-the-week already planned this post, the answers are in the plan (topic, job, reader, platform). Don't ask them again, just confirm the plan line in one sentence and write.

## Rules

- **One reader per post.** If it could be for two different kinds of customer, pick one. A post for everybody lands on nobody.
- **The ask only happens in owned places**: a DM, an email, a call. Never write a public post that tries to close a stranger. If they asked for a closing post on a feed, say why that's the wrong place for it and write the getting-noticed version instead.
- **Specific beats clever.** A real number, a real thing that happened, a real thing somebody said. Cut anything that could have been written by someone who doesn't do this work.
- **Write it the way they'd say it.** If they'd stumble reading it out loud, it's wrong. Contractions. Short words.
- No hashtags. No emojis unless `brand/voice.md` says otherwise.

## Something useful inside the post
Every post gives the reader something they can use even if they never reply: the step, the number, the before and after, the mistake to skip. The ask, when there is one, gives MORE of that, never the only payoff. If the whole value of a post is "DM me", rewrite it until a stranger who never replies still walks away with something.

## Build it in this order
1. **The first line** comes from the write-hooks opener bank, picked for THIS post, and a winner from `knowledge/hooks.md` gets tried first. Write-hooks runs after the draft to sharpen it.
2. **The structure** is one of the five in write-hooks: claim → proof → steps → payoff · question → flat answer → because → verdict · a list with the best one last · a story with the ending first · before → after → the bridge.
3. **Keep the first line's promise open until the end**, and add something new about a third of the way in, so they keep reading.

## Shape it for the platform (the message stays the same)
- **Facebook and groups:** a neighbour talking, not an ad. Short paragraphs, a question at the end, no link in the post if the group frowns on it.
- **LinkedIn:** the first two lines have to earn the "see more" click on their own. One idea, short paragraphs, no link in the post (put it in the first comment).
- **Instagram:** the first line of the caption is the hook. The post itself carries the value (the slide or the video), and the caption backs it up.
- **Threads and X:** one thought per post, in a sentence or two. A second post can carry the link.
- **Email:** one reader, one idea, one link. It reads like a note from the owner, not a newsletter blast.
- **Google Business Profile:** what the business did this week, with a photo, and the one action (call, book, visit).

## Three examples
Made-up businesses from the other lessons, shown so the owner sees the shape land. Never copy them into the owner's posts.
- **Marcus, the roofer (Facebook, give):** "Got a water stain on the ceiling after last week's storm? Before anyone quotes you a new roof, here's the 2-minute check: go in the attic with a flashlight and look right above the stain. Wet wood means it's active, dry and dark means it's old. Most of the stains we get called about turn out to be one cracked boot around a pipe, a few hundred dollars, not a roof. If yours is wet, that's when to call someone, and ask them for photos before a price."
- **Priya, who coaches therapists (LinkedIn, prove):** first two lines: "In January she had an empty calendar and a new practice. By April she had 12 clients, and she never ran an ad." Then the three things she did in order, and the lesson: picking who she helps came before any posting.
- **Hannah, the candle maker (Instagram, give):** caption first line: "Your candle isn't bad. The first burn was too short." Then the rule in one line (let it melt to the edge, about 2 to 3 hours the first time) and why it tunnels if you don't.

## Deliver

Give one post, not three options. Then, under a divider, one line on why it's shaped that way.

If they say it's off, ask what specifically, then, if the miss was about how it sounds, offer the rule to the Editor for `brand/voice.md` and add it only once the owner confirms, marked (owner confirmed). A correction that only lives in the chat helps once. A correction in the file helps every post after it.
