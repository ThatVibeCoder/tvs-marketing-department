# plan-the-week

The week gets planned before anything gets written, so every post has a reason to exist. Then, once the posts are written and approved, the same skill locks them onto the calendar.

## Read first
- `brand/channels.md`: Where You Show Up (the 1 or 2 platforms and the hours) and The Funnel Split. If Where You Show Up still says `_Not filled in yet._`, stop and run the content-brain skill first (the Pick Your Platforms lesson). A week for every platform is a week that doesn't get done.
- `knowledge/topics.md`: the topics and The Four Questions. Empty topics → content-brain first, same reason.
- `knowledge/hooks.md` and the newest `knowledge/reports/content-*.md` if they exist: what won last week gets more of the week.
- `brand/icp.md` and `brand/personas/`, so every post names its reader.

## Say this first
Tell the owner in your own plain words: "I'm going to pick this week's posts from your topic list, three to five of them, each with one reader and one job, and one of them goes out today. You say yes to the plan before anyone writes a word."

## Part 1: Plan the week (before anything is written)
1. **How many.** Count their honest hours from Where You Show Up: under 2 hours a week = 3 posts, 2 to 4 hours = 4, more = 5. Never more than 5 in the first month, because the habit matters more than the volume.
2. **Pick from the topics.** Choose from Topics in `knowledge/topics.md`, never from thin air, and answer The Four Questions for each one: who it's for, give / prove / invite, what they walk away with, where it sends them. A topic marked NEEDS A REAL EXAMPLE only goes in if the owner has the example today.
3. **Weigh the mix** against The Funnel Split. Roughly 7 give / 2 prove / 1 invite means a 4-post week is usually 3 give and 1 prove, and an invite only when the last invite was a while ago. Never two invites back to back.
4. **Spread the platforms** the way Where You Show Up ranks them: most posts on platform 1. If last week's report names a winning opener or topic, give it one slot.
5. **One goes out today.** Mark the easiest one as today's, so the week starts with a post sent, not a plan.

Show the plan on one screen, one line per post: day · platform · topic · job · reader · walks away with. Their yes locks the plan.

Three examples (made-up businesses from the other lessons; never copy them into the owner's files):
- **Marcus, the roofer** (Facebook first, 2 hours): Mon (today) Facebook, "Repair or replace? The 40-photo check", give, homeowners · Wed Facebook, "A 600 dollar repair quoted as a new roof", prove · Fri Google Business Profile, "What a roof check looks like", give.
- **Priya, who coaches therapists** (LinkedIn first, 3 hours): Tue (today) LinkedIn, "Pick who you help before you post", give · Thu LinkedIn, "From agency job to 12 clients", prove · Sat LinkedIn, "Three referral partners every new practice needs", give · Sun email, "The practice-readiness checklist", invite.
- **Hannah, the candle maker** (Instagram first, 3 hours): today Instagram, "How to burn a candle so it lasts", give · Wed Instagram, "A customer's 9th order", prove · Fri email, "October scents are poured", invite · Sun Instagram, "Why candles tunnel", give.

## Part 2: Lock the calendar (after the posts are written and approved)
1. Read brand/channels.md for where you actually post and the effort split.
2. List what content exists and is approved. Never schedule drafts.
3. Lay out the week: what goes where, which day. One post per platform per day at most, unless channels.md says otherwise. Count the mix against the funnel split in brand/channels.md before showing it, and flag any two invitation pieces landing back-to-back.
4. Show the owner the week on one screen. Their yes locks it, and it's saved as `departments/marketing/content-team/social-manager/output/calendar-<YYYY-MM-DD>.md` (the Monday of that week).

When a topic gets used, move its line in `knowledge/topics.md` from Topics to Used with the date and the platform, and add one dated line to that file's Updates.
