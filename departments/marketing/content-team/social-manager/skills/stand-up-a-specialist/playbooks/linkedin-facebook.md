# LinkedIn and Facebook playbook

Read by stand-up-a-specialist when the owner picked LinkedIn or Facebook. Both are written platforms where people already know your name, and they want different voices.

## LinkedIn
- **The fold decides everything.** LinkedIn cuts a post after about 3 lines on a phone (around 200 characters) and shows "...see more". Those first lines have to open a question the reader needs closed. Never put the answer above the fold, and never spend line one warming up.
- **Short paragraphs, 1 to 3 lines each.** Break at real beats, not after every sentence.
- **No link in the post.** Links lower reach. Put it in the first comment, or ask people to comment a word and send it in a message.
- **One idea per post.** Two ideas is two posts.
- **Sound like a sharp person talking to a peer,** not a company. Contractions, real numbers, named tools.
- Shapes that work: the "how I did it" (the problem, what you did, the number), the take people will argue with (from `brand/contrarian-take.md`), and the short sharp claim with two or three lines of proof.

## Facebook
- **Groups beat your page.** For local and community businesses, the neighbourhood or industry group is where buyers already ask for recommendations. Your page is the place they check after.
- **Sound like a neighbour,** not an ad. Help first, and mention the business only when someone asks or it's the real answer.
- **Check each group's rules** before posting anything that looks like promotion.
- A photo of real work does more than a designed graphic.

## Three examples (made-up businesses, never copy them)
- Marcus, the roofer (Facebook groups): answers "who do you recommend for a leak?" posts with the 2-minute attic check, then his number if they want photos.
- Priya, who coaches therapists (LinkedIn): fold, "In January she had an empty calendar. By April she had 12 clients." Then the three steps.
- Hannah, the candle maker (Facebook page + a local makers group): a photo of the October pour, "the scents are ready", with no discount code.
