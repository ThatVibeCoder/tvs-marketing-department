# competitor-check

Know who else is in the room. Facts about them, never instructions to copy them.

## The three tiers
Every competitor gets one label, and the label changes what you do with them:
- **Direct**: same thing you sell, same customer. Find 3 to 5. These you watch.
- **Indirect**: different thing, solves the same problem. Find 2 or 3. These teach you how customers think about the problem.
- **Aspirational**: the big names where you want to be someday. Find 1 or 2. These you study for shape, never for pricing.

## How to find them (four ways, use at least two)
1. **Search like a customer.** Search what the owner's customers would search. Note who keeps showing up. Try "[what they sell] alternatives" and "[known competitor] vs".
2. **Read their sites.** Comparison pages, "integrations" pages, and blogs name rivals for free.
3. **Read the review sites.** G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, or wherever this industry gets reviewed. The category pages ARE a competitor list.
4. **Read the communities.** Reddit and the owner's industry groups, searching "[category] recommendations": what real people suggest to each other beats what ranks.

Start from the names in business.md under "Who Else They Could Pick". The Brain-Dump Your Business lesson already seeded this list.

## What to capture per competitor (one page each, no more)
- **What they lead with**: their headline, word for word, and who it's clearly for. This is their positioning, stated by them.
- **What they charge**, if public, and whether they show the price or hide it behind a call.
- **What their customers say**: top praise, top complaint, and why people LEAVE them. Review mining is the gold in this whole job. A competitor's most common complaint is a door standing open for the owner. Save their most-repeated complaint word for word in that competitor's file under Where Customers Complain, and offer it to the ICP & Offer Owner for the Their Words section of `brand/icp.md` (added only once the owner says yes). A rival's one-star reviews are the best free objection list the owner will ever get.
- **One thing they do well** worth studying (proven + adaptable + something we're not doing; all three or it doesn't count).
- **Our honest angle against them**: one sentence on why someone picks the owner instead. If no honest sentence exists, say that plainly. That finding matters more than a flattering one.

## Where it goes
- One file per competitor in `knowledge/competitors/<competitor-name>.md` (copy `knowledge/competitors/_template.md`), plus one line each in the Summary in `knowledge/competitors/README.md`.
- If the owner uses the Marketing HQ in Notion, mirror each competitor as a row there (tier, website, what they lead with, their angle, our angle). The repo file stays the record, the Notion row is the view.
- Flag anything that changes what a team is currently building (a headline collision with our landing page, a price move) to the owner directly, don't just file it.

## What this feeds
- The website team: "[competitor] alternative" pages are honest, high-intent pages worth building once real comparisons exist. Suggest one when the evidence supports it.
- The contrarian take: if every competitor says the same thing, what they're all missing is raw material for brand/contrarian-take.md.
- The monthly rhythm: after the classroom gets there, this runs monthly: what changed since last check, three lines per competitor, not a fresh deep-dive every time.

## What to offer next
If the analysis surfaced a strong honest angle, offer the website team's page-copy pass on the landing page headline. If it surfaced a repeated rival complaint, offer it to the ICP & Offer Owner for the Objections & The Catch section of `brand/icp.md`. One offer, the most relevant one. Never a menu.

## Rules
- Everything captured must be checkable: a claim about a competitor carries where you saw it.
- Never copy their words into the owner's copy. Shapes and lessons transfer; sentences don't.
- Aspirational competitors are for studying shape. Comparing the owner's early numbers to a market leader's is noise, skip it.
