# Write a Script

A short video lives or dies in its first sentence, and it only earns a share if the viewer walks away with something useful. This writes two or three script options for one topic, each built a different way, so the owner picks the one they'd actually say. The owner walks out with one script picked and ready to record in one take.

## Read first
- `brand/voice.md` (How I Open, Rhythm, Phrases I Use, Words I Never Use). If The Sound still says `_Not filled in yet._`, say so and stop: a script in a voice that isn't theirs reads like an ad. The Round Up Your Own Words lesson fills it.
- `brand/icp.md` and `brand/personas/`: every script is for ONE person.
- The topic: a line from `knowledge/topics.md` the owner picked, or the one "plan my week of content" chose. Answer The Four Questions at the top of that file before writing.
- The opener bank and the five structures in the Copywriter's write-hooks skill (`departments/marketing/content-team/copywriter/skills/write-hooks/SKILL.md`), and `knowledge/hooks.md` for openers that already won.
- The platform's specialist file if one exists (for example `tiktok-specialist.md`): its length and caption rules win.

## Say this first
Tell the owner, in your own plain words: "I'm writing two or three versions of this video, each one built differently. Pick the one you'd actually say out loud. It's about 30 to 45 seconds, and you read it straight through in one take."

## The rules every option follows
1. **Talk to THEM.** "You" and "your" all the way through; "I" shows up once at most. Turn "I did X" into "you can now X".
2. **Sentence one is the whole pitch:** what it is, what it does for them, and the stake, in 9 to 25 words, said by about the 5-second mark. Never "In this video", "Hey guys", "Let me show you" or "Stop scrolling".
3. **Open a question in the first line and answer it only at the end.** That open question is why they keep watching.
4. **Something useful in the middle,** even if they never comment: the step, the number, where to click, the before and after. An ask at the end gives MORE of it, never the only payoff.
5. **Add something new about a third of the way in, and again past halfway,** each time a new piece of value. Filler that only sounds like a hook ("and it gets better", "here's the part nobody tells you") is banned, because the viewer can tell.
6. **Long, linked sentences,** joined with and / so / then / because, the way people really talk: 5 to 9 sentences, 110 to 190 words, about 4 words a second. Never three short sentences in a row.
7. **One payoff line before any ask,** saying what the steps add up to. The ask is optional. Use one only when there's something to give, and keep it to one sentence in the last few seconds.
8. **Never invent** a number, a result or a customer moment. If the script needs one the files don't have, ask the owner for the real one or pick a different structure.

## Write the options
Write 2 or 3 options, each on a different opener AND a different structure from write-hooks (for example a contrarian belief told as a list, then their question answered flat). Above each option, label it in one line:
`OPENER: <name> · STRUCTURE: <name> · THE QUESTION IT OPENS: <...> · NEW VALUE AT: <the two points> · PAYOFF: <the last line's job>`
Then read every option out loud (count the words, check the first sentence lands by about 5 seconds). Cut any line the owner would stumble on.

## Three examples (made-up businesses from the other lessons; never copy them)
- **Marcus, the roofer** (opener: their question · structure: question → flat answer → because → steps → verdict): "Does a water stain on your ceiling mean you need a new roof? Almost never, because most stains come from one small part, and you can check it yourself before anyone quotes you. Grab a flashlight and go up in the attic right above the stain, then look at the wood, because wet and dark means it's leaking now and dry with a ring means it's old. Then look for the pipe coming through the roof nearby, since a cracked rubber boot around it causes most of the leaks we see. So if the wood is dry, you've got time, and if it's wet, get photos from whoever comes out before you get a price."
- **Priya, who coaches therapists** (opener: the contrarian belief · structure: list, best one last): "You don't need a website to fill a new private practice, you need these three things, and the last one is the one nobody does. First, pick the one kind of client you help best, because that's what every referral partner needs to hear. Then make a list of ten people who already see that client, like doctors, school counselors and other therapists, and write one line next to each name about why they'd send someone your way. And finally, ask three of them for a 15-minute coffee this month, because one warm referral partner sends more clients than a year of posting. So the website can wait until you're turning people away, and by then you'll know exactly what it needs to say."
- **Hannah, the candle maker** (opener: their complaint, then the reframe · structure: before → after → the bridge): "'My candles always tunnel.' Your candles are fine, the first burn just set them up to fail. A candle remembers how far it melted the first time, so if you blow it out after 30 minutes, it only ever burns that little circle. Next time, let it burn until the melted wax reaches the edge of the jar, usually 2 to 3 hours. And if one's already tunneled, wrap foil around the top with a hole in the middle and burn it for an hour, because the foil pushes the heat out to the edges. So the fix is time, not a better candle, and every candle you already own just got twice as long a life."

## Where it goes
`departments/marketing/content-team/copywriter/output/scripts-<YYYY-MM-DD>.md`: every option with its label line, the picked one marked PICKED. After the owner records, a note for the take goes in `recordings/<YYYY-MM-DD>-<short-name>.md` (the script, the opener and structure used). The opener goes in `knowledge/hooks.md` under To Try.

## The ending, always
1. Show the options, each with its label line, and ask which one they'd say. One pick, then stop.
2. Tell them how to record it: phone at eye level, read straight through, one take, a second take only if they trip in the first sentence.
3. Save the work with the GitHub Agent's save-work skill (`departments/it-systems/dev-team/github-agent/skills/save-work/SKILL.md`), and tell the owner in one line what changed.

## Done when
The owner has picked one script that talks to one person, opens a question in the first line and answers it at the end, gives something useful in the middle, reads in 30 to 45 seconds, and is saved with its label line.
