# write-hooks

The first line is most of the result. It gets its own pass, every time.

## Steps
1. Read the finished draft and name its one real point in one sentence. The hook comes from the point, never from thin air.
2. Write five candidate first lines, different shapes: the specific number, the reader's own frustration in their words, the honest surprise, the thing that happened, the plain claim. All five true. Pull the shapes from the opener bank below, five different ones, and check `knowledge/hooks.md` first: an opener that already won for this owner goes in the five every time.
3. Kill any that overpromise what the piece delivers. A hook the body can't cash burns trust for every post after it.
4. Read the top two aloud back-to-back. The one you'd actually SAY to a person wins. Show the owner both if it's close. The rest die.
5. Log the pick in `knowledge/hooks.md` under To Try, with the opener's name and the post it went on. When the numbers come back, read-the-stats moves it to Openers That Won or crosses it off.

## Rules
- The hook is written AFTER the piece, not before. You can't promise what doesn't exist yet.
- Specific beats clever: "3 leads from one page" beats "this changed everything."
- Read it in the owner's voice per brand/voice.md. A great hook in the wrong voice is still wrong.

## The opener bank: 10 shapes that work
Each one is a shape, not a script. Fill it from the owner's own files, and never invent the number, the result or the quote it needs. The three examples are made-up businesses (the same three as the other lessons), shown so the owner sees each shape land; never copy them into the owner's work.

1. **The contrarian belief.** "I believe [who] who want [what] should [the different move], not [the common move]." Comes from `brand/contrarian-take.md`.
   Marcus: "Homeowners with a leak should get a 40-photo check, not a new-roof quote from the driveway." · Priya: "Therapists who want a full practice should pick who they help before they post anything." · Hannah: "Stop buying better candles. Start burning the ones you have the right way."
2. **Their question, read out loud.** Open with the exact question a buyer asks, then answer it flat in the next sentence.
   Marcus: "Does a leak always mean a new roof? No, and most of the time it's a 600 dollar fix." · Priya: "Do you need a website before your first private client? No. You need three referral partners." · Hannah: "Why does my candle burn a hole down the middle? Because the first burn was too short."
3. **Their complaint, then the reframe.** Say the common complaint in general (never one person's words), then break it in one line.
   Marcus: "'Roofers always find more damage once they're up there.' Only the ones who didn't take photos first." · Priya: "'Marketing feels gross.' That's because you're marketing to everyone." · Hannah: "'Candles never smell as strong at home.' It's the room, not the candle."
4. **Result first.** The outcome and a real number before any setup, so "how?" becomes their question.
   Marcus: "Three repairs this month that other companies quoted as full roofs." · Priya: "Twelve clients in 90 days, and she never ran an ad." · Hannah: "One customer has ordered nine times this year."
5. **The time contract.** "[Short time] is all you need to [specific outcome]." Only when the time is honest.
   Marcus: "Ten minutes in your attic tells you if that stain is new." · Priya: "One afternoon is all you need to find your first three referral partners." · Hannah: "Two hours on the first burn is all it takes to make a candle last twice as long."
6. **The warning list.** "Don't [common action] until you've [done these N things]." A warning plus a number opens two questions at once.
   Marcus: "Don't sign a roof contract until you've asked these 3 questions." · Priya: "Don't buy a practice-building course until you can answer these 4 questions." · Hannah: "Don't light a new candle until you've done these 2 things."
7. **Most people… but you can.** How most people solve it, then the better way, named but not explained yet.
   Marcus: "Most people get three quotes, but the photos tell you more than the price." · Priya: "Most new private practices start with a website, but the ones that fill up start with a list of ten people." · Hannah: "Most people trim the wick when it smokes, but the fix is how long you let it burn the first time."
8. **The cost line.** What NOT knowing this costs them, in money, hours or customers. Describe the situation, never mock the person.
   Marcus: "Every month a small leak waits is drywall you'll pay for twice." · Priya: "Every month you market to everyone is a month your ideal client doesn't know you exist." · Hannah: "Every candle that tunnels is half a candle you paid for and never burned."
9. **Kill a cost.** "You don't need to pay for [the thing] anymore." The next line stays on THEM: what they do instead.
   Marcus: "You don't need a full roof to fix one bad valley." · Priya: "You don't need a branding agency to get your first ten clients." · Hannah: "You don't need a wick trimmer if you burn it right the first time."
10. **Visual first.** The opening frame does the work: the before and after, the thing in their hand, the number on a screen. The words follow what they're already looking at.
   Marcus: the water stain, then the photo of the cracked flashing above it. · Priya: her calendar, empty in January and full in April. · Hannah: two candles side by side, one tunneled and one burned even.

## The five structures a post or script hangs on
The opener is line one. These are what comes after it, so the first line has something to pay off:
1. **Claim → name and proof → you-steps → payoff.** For a method or a tool: what it is, why it's real, what it does for THEM step by step, what it adds up to.
2. **Question → flat answer → because → steps → verdict.** Pairs with opener 2 and 3.
3. **List, best one last.** A number in the opener, one item per line, the strongest saved for the end.
4. **Story: the ending first → the problem → what happened → the lesson.** For a real job or a real customer moment, told as a situation, never quoting the customer.
5. **Before → after → the bridge.** Show the after, show the before, then the exact thing that got them across.
Whatever the structure, keep the promise of line one open until the end, and add something new about a third of the way in, so they keep reading.
